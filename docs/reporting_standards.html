Reporting Standards

These standards apply to every brief, report, and final project in ENV 730. Every deliverable is graded as scientific writing, not as a folder of outputs. A plot with no sentence, or a question left unanswered, is incomplete.

ImportantThe one rule everything else follows

You make the entire argument in your writing. Figures and citations are there to support that argument — they never stand in for it. The text carries the reasoning from start to finish; a plot or a citation is evidence you point to, not a substitute for saying what you found.

Four standards for every submission

Standard What it means
Make the argument in the text The whole argument lives in your prose, in complete sentences. Figures and citations support it — raw output, bullet fragments, or code alone is not a submission.
Answer the question with evidence Every question gets a direct claim, backed by a specific number or figure. Restating the question, or showing a plot without a conclusion, does not answer it.
Caption every figure and table Give each one a number and a caption that says what it shows and the takeaway, so it stands on its own away from the code.
Cite your figures and sources in the text Point to the support explicitly (“Figure 1 shows…”). Every figure, table, and reference must be mentioned in the writing where it backs your argument.

Answer the question: make a claim, then prove it

A question is answered when you state a conclusion and support it with evidence the reader can check.

  • State the claim in a sentence — the answer, not a description of the plot.
  • Back it with a specific value or a labelled figure, cited by number.
  • Do not leave the reader to infer it from a chart — say what the chart shows.
  • If two things differ, say which is larger, by how much, and where you see it.
TipWeak vs. strong

Weak: a temperature plot, no text.

Strong: “New Haven is milder in winter — mean January tmean is 2.4 °C versus −3.1 °C at Yale-Myers (Fig. 2).”

Every figure earns a caption and a mention

A figure is evidence only if the reader knows what it is and you point them to it.

  • Number every figure and table (Figure 1, Figure 2, Table 1).
  • The caption states what it shows plus the one takeaway, and reads on its own.
  • Label axes with units — a bare plot is not yet evidence.
  • Mention each figure by number in the text. No orphan figures.
NoteA caption that works

Figure 1. Monthly mean temperature with standard error for Yale-Myers and New Haven, 1990–2018. New Haven is warmer in every month, and the gap is largest in winter.

What we are fixing this term

Common problem The fix
Code and figures, but no text Write prose that interprets the results and answers each question.
Figures with no captions Add a numbered caption stating what it shows and the takeaway.
Figures never mentioned in the writing Cite each one by number where it supports your point (“Fig. 3…”).
The question is restated but not answered Give a direct claim and the evidence behind it, not just the setup.

Before you submit: a quick self-check

These standards apply to every deliverable in the course. When in doubt, ask: does the text make the argument, and does every figure and citation earn its place in support of it?
